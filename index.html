About

An analyst learning to explain the whole event

I am Sudersen Lekshmikanth, a security operations analyst and a WQE7001 Cyber Security student at Universiti Malaya. My current work includes reviewing security logs and alerts across network, endpoint and identity tools. I want to become better at tracing an alert back to the source events, checking the conditions behind it and explaining what still needs investigation.

Cybersecurity interests me because a clear finding depends on both technical detail and careful judgement. A service banner, for example, can point to a question worth checking, but it does not prove that a system is vulnerable. The projects here use systems I own in an isolated lab. They document the setup, the saved output and what I can reasonably conclude from each step. My longer-term direction is L2 incident handling, with stronger evidence review and more useful handovers.

Learning journey

What changed in my approach

The course has pushed me to connect a technical observation to its conditions, evidence and limits.

In the Week 1 vulnerability exercise, I compared cases from industrial, energy, healthcare and public-sector systems. The CVE identifier alone was not enough to assess them. The useful question was more specific: which product and version were affected, what access would be needed, and what action would actually reduce the risk? The Unitronics controller example showed why a default password and internet exposure must be considered together. That exercise gave me a more useful way to read an advisory. I now look for the affected asset and the exposure condition before making a claim about likely impact.

The private VMware lab in my earlier pathway assignment made scope tangible. Kali Linux and a Metasploitable 2 target shared one LAN segment. I checked the enabled adapters, the assigned addresses and the routes before scanning. Kali had only the connected 192.168.77.0/24 route; an outside-lab route lookup returned “Network is unreachable.” I also recorded a successful three-packet ping to the owned target. The lesson was that saying “isolated” is weaker than showing the adapter settings and route table that supported the boundary during the run.

One setup step did go wrong. The old target console did not accept automated text, so the static address could not be entered through that route. A snapshot and configuration backup were made before using a temporary host-only management path. After the address was set, the extra path was removed and both VMs were checked on the original private segment. The later Nmap result therefore came from the intended lab network. This taught me to keep a record of a workaround and verify the final state, rather than assuming that the workaround left nothing behind.

The service scan raised a different problem: interpretation. Nmap reported FTP, SSH, HTTP and an SMB service label on four selected ports. Its Samba version was a range, and the other banners identified reported software rather than confirmed vulnerabilities. I can use these observations to decide what to check next, but I cannot turn them into a finding of exploitation. I still find it challenging to connect a version estimate to a specific package, configuration and CVE condition without jumping ahead of the evidence.

By 31 May 2027, I want to complete four further authorised lab investigations. For each, I will keep the original output, write a one-page note that separates observations from hypotheses, and identify one additional record that would confirm or challenge the explanation. I will review those notes against the source evidence and seek feedback on whether another analyst could repeat my reasoning. That is a measurable step toward the L2 incident-handling work I want to do.

Career objective

Toward L2 incident handling

My target role is an L2 security operations analyst who can investigate an alert across source logs, endpoint observations and network context, then give the next responder a clear account of what is known and what remains open. I already work with monitoring tools in an L1 role. My next step is to get more deliberate about the evidence behind each alert.

First, I will practise tracing alert conditions back to the original records and time window in controlled examples. Second, I will continue small, authorised labs that let me document a method and test an explanation against saved output. Third, I will build stronger case notes and ask for feedback on their clarity and limits. I plan to study toward CompTIA CySA+, with a readiness review before deciding when to sit the exam.
